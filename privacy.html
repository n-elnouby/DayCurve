Privacy Policy

DayCurve: Caffeine & Body Clock · Effective 4 October 2026

Short version: DayCurve has no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Everything you log stays on your iPhone and Apple Watch. The developer never receives your data.

Who is responsible

The controller is noubyte, the developer of DayCurve. The developer's name and postal address are shown on DayCurve's App Store page in the EU. Contact: noubyte@absan.de.

Data stored on your device

Your caffeine log, favourite drinks, questionnaire answers, chronotype, sleep and wake times, trips and settings are stored locally on your iPhone and Apple Watch. They are not uploaded to any server run by the developer, and DayCurve does not sync them to iCloud. Device backups you make with Apple (iCloud or computer) include app data under Apple's terms.

Apple Health

With your permission DayCurve reads sleep, heart rate, heart-rate variability and caffeine from Apple Health, and can save the caffeine you log to Apple Health. Health data is used only inside the app: to estimate your chronotype and body clock, to show how caffeine relates to your sleep, and to include caffeine logged in other apps. It is never used for advertising, never sold and never shared with third parties. You can change or revoke access at any time in the Health app (Sharing → Apps → DayCurve).

GainCurve

If you also use GainCurve, our fitness app, and switch the link on in both apps, DayCurve shares your recent caffeine doses, caffeine half-life, planned bedtime, limits and its summary of your sleep (last night, your sleep need and sleep debt) with GainCurve, and GainCurve shares your recent workouts, your usual training time and the time and calories of each day's last meal with DayCurve. This happens on your iPhone only, through storage shared by the two apps; nothing is uploaded, and you can switch it off in Settings at any time.

Apple Watch

The iPhone and Apple Watch apps exchange your caffeine log and settings directly with each other through Apple's WatchConnectivity. Nothing passes through the developer.

Barcodes

When you scan a drink's barcode, the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts to look up the product. No account, name, location or identifier about you is sent with the request, but like any internet request it can see your IP address. Open Food Facts' own privacy policy applies: openfoodfacts.org/privacy. The built-in drink catalog works without any network request.

Camera and notifications

The camera is used only to read barcodes; no pictures are stored. Notifications (the last call before your cut-off, planned caffeine times, jet-lag plan steps) are created and delivered on your device.

In-app purchases

DayCurve Pro and trip passes are sold through Apple's App Store. Apple handles payment and your Apple ID; DayCurve only asks the App Store on your device what you have bought and receives no payment or account details. See Apple's privacy policy for how Apple processes purchases.

Legal basis (GDPR)

Processing happens on your device at your request to provide the app's features (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); Apple Health data is only accessed with your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw in the Health app at any time. Because the developer does not receive your data, requests for access or deletion can be fulfilled by you directly in the app (the caffeine log can be exported with DayCurve Pro) or by deleting it. You have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority.

Not medical advice

DayCurve gives general information about caffeine, sleep timing and jet lag. It is not a medical device and does not give medical advice.

Children

DayCurve is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
